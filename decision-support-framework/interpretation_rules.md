# Interpretation Rules

This file explains what we are allowed to say about the data.

The goal is simple: do not overstate the evidence.

## Questions To Answer

Before writing page text, answer:

- Are we describing what happened?
- Are we predicting what might happen?
- Are we diagnosing why something happened?
- Are we recommending an action?
- Are we claiming cause and effect?

## Rules

- Do not say something caused an outcome unless we tested cause and effect.
- Do not combine separate findings into an "ideal profile" unless combinations were measured.
- Do not describe model scores as certainty.
- Do not hide important filters, exclusions, or sample-size rules.
- Use business language people actually use.

## Safe Wording

Use wording like:

- "More common among"
- "Less common among"
- "Overrepresented compared with"
- "Underrepresented compared with"
- "Associated with"
- "Worth reviewing"
- "Needs deeper inspection"

## Wording To Avoid Unless Proven

Avoid wording like:

- "Causes"
- "Guarantees"
- "Best"
- "Ideal profile"
- "Always"
- "Predicts with certainty"

## Rule

The page should sound as confident as the evidence allows, and no more.
